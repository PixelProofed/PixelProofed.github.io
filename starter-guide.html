[hr] Every character begins with a choice what kind of life they are entering this world with. Some arrive from other worlds as strangers, some have lived here from birth while carrying the echoes of another life, and some begin as actual monsters with paths of evolution ahead of them. Your character type determines which sheet you use, how many starting stat points you receive, what traits you begin with, how many skills you start with, and which reference pages matter most during creation. The character types are Transmigrator, Reincarnator, Reincarnated Monster, and Native. //Transmigrators// These are humans taken from another world and brought here exactly as they were. A Transmigrator must come from a world without a system, monsters, or any supernatural forces. They enter this setting as true outsiders, unfamiliar with its rules, its dangers, and the structure of life here. They begin with an even 10 in every stat and receive 20 additional stat points to distribute freely. They will have two special traits granted by Siras, have a maximum stat total of 800, and begin with one notable skill, also directly gifted by Siras. While the skill can be crafted by you, the traits will be rolled for in the **#Rolls** channel on the server, with a 4d100.[hr] - **--Base 10 in each stat, plus 20 points to distribute.--** - **--Start at level 4, with a max stat total of 800.--** - **--Start with 1 special skill and two random traits.--** //Reincarnators// These characters were born and raised in this world, but carry the faint or vivid remains of a previous life. They are not outsiders in the same way a Transmigrator is. They know the world around them because it has always been their home, even if something older lingers beneath that life. This aside, their past life still needs to have been as a human from another world without magic or anything supernatural. There are multiples Races to choose from for Reincarnators. They begin with 100 stat points to distribute, use the listed traits and stat total of their race, and begin with three skills. [hr] - **--100 stat points to distribute.--** - **--Start level and max stat total varies by race.--** - **--Start with 3 basic skills and two race traits.--** //Reincarnator Monsters// These characters begin as monsters. They use the monster sheet and will start in a weak origin form, while in exchange they gain access to monster evolution lines and the added traits those evolutions provide over time. Like other Reincarnators, they may remember a past life to whatever degree suits the character, again needing to have been a human from a normal world in that past life. They must have been 18+ when they died, due to monsters having different aging rates. They begin with 50 stat points to distribute, start with three skills, and have a maximum stat total of 800.[hr] - **--50 stat points to distribute.--** - **--Start at level 1 with a max stat total of 800.--** - **--Start with 3 basic skills and one species trait.--** //Natives// These are people wholly of this world, born into it without any past-life memory. They know its customs, its dangers, and its systems as ordinary facts of life. They use the humanoid sheet, begin with three skills, and use the traits and stat total listed for their race.[hr] - **--100 stat points to distribute.--** - **--Start level and max stat total varies by race.--** - **--Start with 3 basic skills and two race traits.--**

[hr] Stats are an aspect granted by the world's system that amplify and measure a character's ability across five major areas of performance: physical power, speed, intellect, force of personality, and endurance. They affect combat, system rolls, profession activities, and many other checks throughout play. Some stats are broad in what they influence, while others come up more heavily in specific systems. Due to stats being part of the world's System, they do not necessarily reflect a character's physical body. For example, someone with high STR does not have to be super muscled, they will be strong regardless. The cap per individual stats is 200. Buffs can raise a stat up to 300. In this leveling system, level is calculated from how many stat points a character has, total. 50 Stats points is the start of level 1, while different races have different max stat totals for level 100. This means different races will level at different rates. **Strength** Strength reflects raw physical power. It affects melee force, lifting capacity, and other feats that rely on direct bodily power. Characters with high Strength may hit harder, carry more, and perform better in tasks that depend on force rather than finesse. **Agility** Agility reflects coordination, reflexes, reaction time, and overall bodily control. It affects evasion, balance, accuracy, and how well a character can respond to sudden danger or act cleanly under pressure. Agility is one of the most common stats used in rolls involving traps. **Intellect** Intellect reflects reasoning, learning, magical understanding, and general mental acuity. It affects problem-solving, strategy, magical knowledge, and the ability to understand or work through more complex systems. It may also be used in place of Agility for certain rolls involving magical traps. **Charisma** Charisma reflects force of presence and social influence. It affects persuasion, leadership, deception, charm, and similar social interactions. It also plays into the Charisma Action system and improves a character's ability to barter. **Endurance** Endurance reflects stamina, resilience, and physical staying power. It affects how much punishment a character can take, how long they can keep going, and how well they hold up under strain. Endurance also determines stamina used in certain minigame systems, making it especially helpful to profession characters. //What The Numbers Mean// These values are not separate tiers of progression so much as rough benchmarks for how a stat should be understood in play. The default play style here is freeform outside of certain mechanics, so the information here is meant to provide a general understanding to work with. **10 — Average Human** The level of an ordinary person without exceptional training. A character at this level can manage normal daily life, but is not especially competitive in demanding situations. **50 — Trained** Represents solid training, natural talent, or both. A character at this level is clearly capable and performs well in their chosen area. **100 — Expert** A stat at this level reflects exceptional ability. The character stands out clearly from the ordinary and can be considered highly skilled in that area. **150 — Master** This level represents someone whose ability is widely recognized. A character here can accomplish things that most trained people cannot reliably match. **200 — Peak** This is the natural peak for distributed stats. A character at this level represents the highest end of natural development without outside enhancement. **300 — Legendary** This level is only reached through buffs, artifacts, or other outside enhancements. It goes beyond natural limits and represents truly extreme performance, even if temporary. //Mechanical Usage// **Stat Modifiers** The modifier system is simple: a stat provides +1 to rolls using it, per 50 points. **Endurance and Stamina** Endurance affects stamina in minigame systems such as mining, gathering, and scavenging, and fishing. Characters begin with 50 stamina, and gain 50 more for every 50 points in END. **Trap Rolls** Agility is the most common stat used for finding, dodging, or reacting to traps. Magical traps may call for INT instead. **Debuff Resistance** Different debuffs call for different resisting stats to be used as modifiers. It varies a fair amount between debuffs. **Charisma Systems** Charisma affects more than dialogue flavor. It is used in the charisma action system and also improves bartering. The Charisma Actions system involves the use of a Leverage resource, determined by your CHA. - Your character will have 1 Leverage to start, with +1 per 50 in CHA, and it can be spent per RP to use charisma effects. These are not mind control, rather ways to change the shape of a social situation. Those with a Charm trait have access to a deeper Leverage system that may get much closer to mind control. **Other Minigame Effects** Several stats influence profession and minigame systems in smaller ways, depending on the specific activity. Fishing make use of AGI and STR, Bartering makes use of CHA, Scavenging ranks require INT, etc.

[hr] Traits are special passive abilities granted through the System that shape what a character is capable of. There's an extreme amount of variation in traits across the world's races, Transmigrators, and Monsters. Some may simply grant stat bonuses, others grant abilities or advantages, and others still might have a major effect on how a character approaches the world. Each race bprm into the world has a predefined set of two traits. Every individual born into each race will have the same two traits. This is not the case for Transmigrators. As Transmigrators were not born in this world, they are granted their two traits directly by the world's god, Siras. OOC, this is done by rolling a set of four traits from a list of a hundred, and choosing two of them. The trait list is intentionally written in a brief and flexible way. The description of the trait is the part that matters most, while the exact way it shows up in your character can be interpreted with some freedom. When building your character, you are welcome to describe how a trait works for them in practice, so long as that interpretation still makes sense for the trait itself and doesn't work in extra advantages the trait doesn't actually cover. For stat bonus traits, the written effect still comes first, while the stat increase is simply an added benefit. There is a wide range of power among the available traits. Some are straightforward, some are highly situational or story-based, and some are just extremely strong in general. A trait that looks simple on paper may still become very effective depending on how you use it. Some traits are limited and operate on a first-come, first-serve basis. If you roll one of these and it has already been claimed, you may choose another non-limited trait instead. While Transmigrators have the larger trait list of a hundred potential rolls, Reincarnators/Natives will find their traits on the Races page, and Monsters will find their traits in the Monster Guide. Monsters are also unique with traits in that, even though they start with one, they gain another during each evolution and will have 4 traits by level 80, varying depending on the evolution path they took.

[hr] Skills are active abilities enforced by the system. They are not general actions, broad talents, or passive qualities. A skill is something a character deliberately uses, more like a named technique or special move than a flexible form of control. In simple terms, think Fireball, not fire control. Because of that, skills are meant to be narrow and specific. A skill should usually be one clear action, or at most a small sequence of actions tied together into a single use. They are not meant to cover an entire fighting style, an entire school of magic, or a constant state of enhancement. If something is written as passive, ongoing, or innate, it is not a skill. Every skill should make sense for the character who has it. For most characters, starting skills should be relatively basic and fit the life they have actually lived up to that point. A young fighter might begin with a lunging strike, a hunter with a precise shot, and a novice mage with a simple elemental attack. Starting skills should feel like techniques a character could reasonably know at the beginning of play, not fully developed signature arts. Transmigrators are the one exception. Their first skill is granted by Siras on arrival, which allows it to be a little stranger, more game-like, or more system-oriented than normal. These starting skills do not have to be stronger, but they can include ideas often associated with isekai protagonists, such as Appraisal, Mini-Map, or Inventory. That extra freedom only applies to the Transmigrator's starting skill, not to any skill they learn afterward. Monsters have one special exception of their own. Monsters can be born with skills, and may be born with a magic-based skill that makes sense for its species, even if it does not have the matching Affinity trait. This and Evolution Skills are the only ways for a monster without an affinity to gain a magic skill, outside of obtaining a dungeon scroll. Even then, it should remain basic, species-appropriate, and is limited to a single magic skill if the monster has no affinity. //What Skills Are Not// - They are not passive effects. - They are not broad powers like total elemental control. - They are not meant to replace ordinary actions or roleplay entirely. - They are not usually very flexible in what they do once defined. //Starting Skill Concepts// - A direct strike - A short dash or lunge - A simple elemental attack - A defensive reaction - A basic support technique - A small utility action with a clear function The Skills page will contain full information on guidelines, restrictions, and examples. It will also be where you can find information on Debuffs, Buffs, Magic in general, and Charisma-based actions.

[hr] The Races page contains the full list of Races, Humans and Demihumans, along with their racial traits, max stat totals, and general in-world information. It is used for both Reincarnated and Native characters. The only difference between a Reincarnator and a Native is that Reincarnators carry memories from a past life, while Natives are wholly of this world and do not. Each race is built with its own strengths, racial buffs/debuffs, and maximum stat totals. Because of that, some races are generally more powerful, while others have more advantage in things like crafting. The difference in the Max Stat Totals between races can ultimately be overcome by anyone determined enough, with the NG+ system. When a Native/Reincarnator reaches level 100, they may choose to start over from level 1 and gain +100 to their max stat total, as well as roll a 1d50 for a trait to replace one of their current ones. Skills and Proffession progress will be kept. This can be done multiple times, until they reach a Max Stat Total of 800. The weaknesses listed in a race entry are there to help communicate the feel and identity of that race. They are flavor guidance, not separate mechanical penalties, unless otherwise stated. There are no gender-locked races. Any race that might traditionally be treated as male-only or female-only can be played as any gender.

Quick Overview

[hr] - To start, you'll select one of the four character type: Transmigrator, Reincarnator, Reincarnated Monster, or Native. - If Transmigrator, you'll roll for your two traits in the #Rolls channel, and come up with a skill. - If Reincarnator or Native, you'll decide on a race from the Races page, and come up with three skills. - If Monster, you'll decide on a starter species from the Evolution Trees page, and come up with three skills. - Then, it's just a matter of filling in your personality and backstory!
